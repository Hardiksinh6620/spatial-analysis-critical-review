# Spatial reasoning and scope

The report's opening two pages frame spatial analysis around location, distribution, and relationships. This reading guide separates three questions:

| Question | Required evidence | Typical output |
|---|---|---|
| Where are features? | Located observations with an understood geographic reference | A distribution map |
| How are features related? | A stated relationship such as adjacency or distance | Neighbourhoods or proximity summaries |
| Why might a pattern occur? | Relevant variables and a defensible analytical design | An explanation with uncertainty |

A map can reveal a pattern worth investigating without establishing its cause. Decide the study area, observation unit, and data period before choosing a technique. The report introduces a range of techniques; mentioning them does not mean that every technique was implemented.
