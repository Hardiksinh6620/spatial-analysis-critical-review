# Spatial and non-spatial questions

The comparison on PDF pages 2-3 is useful as a starting point. A practical distinction is whether the question needs a relationship between locations; the subject domain alone does not determine that.

| Example question | Non-spatial summary | Added spatial question |
|---|---|---|
| How many facilities exist? | Count and category totals | Which neighbourhoods have fewer nearby facilities? |
| What is the average observed value? | Mean and distribution | Do nearby observations have similar values? |
| What does a service cost? | Cost totals by service type | How does travel or location affect that cost? |

Use both forms of evidence when needed. A geographic identifier in a table does not automatically make every calculation spatial. Conversely, a health or finance question can be spatial when neighbourhood relationships are part of the design.

Before comparing areas, record their boundaries, population denominators, and observation periods. Differences in those choices can change what the comparison means.
